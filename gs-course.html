The questions
aspirants actually ask.

01I have given several attempts and my GS score has barely moved. Why would this be different?
Because a stuck score is usually a feedback problem, not a knowledge problem. Nobody has told you which specific mistakes you keep repeating. Here your subject-end tests are reviewed, your doubts are answered live every week, and the syllabus moves in a fixed sequence instead of wherever your motivation takes you that day. We cannot promise a result. We can promise you will know exactly where you stand and why.
02How soon should I expect my GS to improve?
You should feel the difference in your routine inside the first week, because there is finally a fixed rhythm to follow. Scores are slower, and we will be honest about that. The first real signal is your subject-end mock, and the clearest signal comes after the revision cycles. Anyone promising a jump in a fortnight is selling you something.
03I am starting from scratch and the syllabus looks enormous. Can I still join?
Yes, and starting fresh is often easier than unlearning a scattered preparation. The course moves one topic a day, in order, so you are never asked to hold the whole syllabus in your head. You only ever have to do today's topic. Ninety of those, and the syllabus is behind you.
04Is the ₹999 trial really the way to start? What is the catch?
There is no catch. Two weeks of Geography for ₹999, with the full system running exactly as it does for enrolled students: the notes, the lectures, the doubt session, the test. It is priced low rather than free because a free trial fills up with browsers, and this one is meant for people who intend to work. If it is not for you, you simply do not continue.
05How is this different from free lectures and PDFs?
Free content hands you information and walks away. It cannot tell you what you got wrong, answer the doubt you have this week, or hold you to a routine when you slip. This course is taught, tested and mentored, so you are guided rather than merely supplied. That is the whole difference, and it is the difference that shows up in a score.
06How much time does this need every day?
Plan for roughly two hours on a normal weekday: the notes the previous evening, the lecture in the morning, and a short revision of what you covered. Test days and the pre-exam revision block ask for more. The point is not to study longer than you already do. It is to make the hours you already spend land somewhere.
07I am working full time. Can I keep up with the live batch?
Many working aspirants do, because the lecture is at 7 AM and the notes arrive the previous evening. If your hours are unpredictable, the Self-Paced plan is the honest recommendation: the same 52 lectures, notes and mocks, on your own schedule. Take the ₹999 trial first and see which one your week can actually hold.
08What happens after I start the trial?
As soon as your ₹999 is confirmed, Anmol messages you personally on WhatsApp with your access and the schedule for the two weeks. The lectures and structured notes are delivered through your VicThree student login, the notes arrive the evening before each class, and the live doubt-solving session is shared with your batch. You are never left to work out the logistics on your own.
